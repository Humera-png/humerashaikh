Every founder eventually hits the same wall: the team feels slower than it used to, and the instinctive fix is to hire. More engineers, more support staff, more hands. It rarely works the way anyone hopes.

That's because most slowdowns aren't a headcount problem. They're a systems problem wearing a headcount costume.

## The tell

If a new hire would spend their first month untangling the same manual process everyone already knows is broken, you don't have a capacity problem. You have a process that doesn't scale past the person who built it.

Hiring into a broken system doesn't fix the system. It just adds another person who now also has to work around it.

## What actually moves the needle

Before adding a seat, I ask a founder to walk me through what happens between a request coming in and it being done. Almost every time, there's a step that's manual because nobody's had time to automate it, not because it's actually hard to automate.

That step is usually where the real leverage is. Fix it once, and it stays fixed for every person who touches that workflow afterward, including the ones you haven't hired yet.

## The reframe

"We need more people" is a conclusion. The useful question underneath it is: *what is slow, specifically, and why does it require a human every single time?*

Answer that first. Sometimes you still need to hire. But you'll hire for growth instead of hiring to compensate for debt, and that's a much better position to scale from.
